---
title: N4, processes and scheduling, is met
description: Processes, the scheduler, signals, terminals, the clock and the arm64 trap and system-call entry now live in the safe kernel crate and in hal.
---
Milestone N4 closed on 2026-10-24, tagged
[`n4-processes`]({{ site.repo }}/releases/tag/n4-processes). In seventeen slices, from
2026-10-21 to 2026-10-24, processes and scheduling left the LandingZone tree:

- **Into the `kernel` crate**, which the compiler refuses to build with any `unsafe`: timeouts
  and tasks, processes, credentials, fork and exit, resource limits and accounting, the file
  descriptors, kqueue, select and poll, signals, futex, the scheduler, sysctl's core, the
  message buffer, terminals and pseudo-terminals, the timecounters and the clock.
- **Into `hal`**, the only crate allowed `unsafe`, each site with a written proof: the
  context switch, spl and soft interrupts, the kernel lock, arm64's timer, exception vectors,
  trap and system-call entry, the user copies, signal delivery, the Spectre-BHB trampolines
  and kernel page-table isolation.

105 of the 331 system calls are now handled natively, from 17 when N4 started. The whole test
suite passed on arm64, on two and on four CPUs, with the installer end to end, and the
comparison with a real OpenBSD found no new difference.

The cost is measured too: under QEMU on arm64, the page fault is 41 % slower than at N3, pipe
35 %, fork plus exec 24 %, fork plus exit 19 %, a file read 11 %. Correctness comes first;
those numbers go to N12, the performance milestone.

Next is N5: the VFS and the file systems. The [Status]({{ '/status/' | relative_url }}) page has
the details.
